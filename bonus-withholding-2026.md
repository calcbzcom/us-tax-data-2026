# Bonus Withholding Examples (2026)

How federal withholding works on a bonus paid as supplemental wages. Figures follow the flat-rate method from IRS Publication 15 (2026) and assume the employee is below the $184,500 Social Security wage base and lives in a state with no wage income tax (for example Florida).

| Bonus | Federal income tax (22%) | Social Security (6.2%) | Medicare (1.45%) | Estimated take-home |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1,000 | $220.00 | $62.00 | $14.50 | $703.50 |
| $5,000 | $1,100.00 | $310.00 | $72.50 | $3,517.50 |
| $10,000 | $2,200.00 | $620.00 | $145.00 | $7,035.00 |
| $25,000 | $5,500.00 | $1,550.00 | $362.50 | $17,587.50 |

## Notes

- The flat rate is 22%, and 37% on supplemental wages above $1 million in a calendar year.
- Some employers instead add the bonus to regular pay and withhold on the total.
- Withholding is a prepayment, not the final tax. The real amount is settled on the tax return.
